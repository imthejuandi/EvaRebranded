# Routing and brief templates

Use only the fields the request warrants. Leave unknown facts unknown.

## Task contract

- **Intent and action level:** What outcome is the user or authorized standing instruction asking for? Answer, explore, draft, do, or monitor?
- **Inputs and destination:** What sources, project state, audience or artifact govern the answer? Where does the result or action belong?
- **Boundaries:** What must remain unchanged, what is not authorized, what decision belongs to the user, and what can proceed independently?
- **Success:** What could an observer verify? What would justify no change? What evidence or approval is needed to proceed?
- **Execution:** Choose direct, scoped or orchestrated. Use the lowest cost and latency that preserves reliability. Choose model/effort and skills by task, risk, capability and availability, not a permanent roster.

## Specialist assignment

“You own [question or deliverable], using [available model/effort, skills, specific inputs]. Do not change [non-goals]. Return [format], with [evidence and acceptance test], by [dependency or deadline if relevant]. Surface [stop conditions]. Do not infer missing authorization.”

For the plan reviewer: give the proposed plan, evidence, limits and user goal. Ask for consequential omissions, redundant roles, and sequencing risks. Record what changed. For the final reviewer: give the exact artifact and appropriate checks, not the author's verdict; for comprehension, capture neutral observations before sharing the rubric. If two rounds produce no material gain, reassess rather than repeat by default.

## Examples that exercise different routes

- **“What’s the difference between these two options?”** Direct or scoped depending on evidence. Answer in the requested format, verify decision-changing facts and name which you would pick when useful. No agent for a simple comparison.
- **“Rewrite this investor email, don’t send it.”** Scoped draft. Find the recipient context, preserve factual claims and tone, deliver a clean copyable draft. No send check or authorization implied.
- **“Check whether we can submit to this grant.”** Scoped research or orchestrated if independent eligibility, finance and operations workstreams truly matter. Pin official criteria and evidenced company facts, report eligible/ineligible/unknown and the exact blockers. No submission just because the check passed.
- **“Change this existing website.”** Use the relevant project skill and current baseline. A one-line correction may be scoped. A multilingual release candidate may need separate specialists and independent render checks. No publishing from a preview-only brief.
- **Assistant idea: “We could monitor this recurring expense.”** Verify why it matters and whether existing authority covers checking; propose or set up the authorized check as appropriate. The idea itself does not authorize account changes, payments, or contacting anyone.
